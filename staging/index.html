The road we are building

Fifty hours is a long road. We help families travel it together.

Many states require teens to complete supervised practice before earning a license. Drive Venture helps teens and their grown-ups record that experience, understand what remains, and make room for the varied practice that builds confidence behind the wheel.

Starting in Michigan: Drive Venture currently supports families working toward Michigan’s 50 supervised hours, including 10 hours at night.

Why all that practice?

The practice is the safety margin.

New drivers face their greatest crash risk during their first months driving alone. Supervised practice gives teens time to build real-road experience while a grown-up is there to coach, anticipate hazards, and intervene.

01

More practice, fewer serious events

In a major 2025 U.S. study, the higher-practice group later experienced about 12 crashes and near-crashes per 1,000 independent-driving hours, compared with 17 in the lower-practice group.

02

Variety builds experience

Daylight is only one route. Darkness, weather, traffic, road types, and unfamiliar trips give learners appropriately varied experience before they drive alone.

03

A log reveals what is missing

Tracking conditions as well as time helps families find gaps—such as plenty of daylight driving but little experience at dusk or on multilane roads.

What that finding means: The higher-practice group had about 29% fewer observed crashes and near-crashes per hour of independent driving. That is compelling evidence to take supervised practice seriously, but it is not a guarantee and does not prove that exactly 50 hours caused the difference.
